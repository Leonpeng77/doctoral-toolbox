APA 6 to APA 7: what actually changed

If your reference manager still thinks it is 2009, this is the list of things that will be wrong. Most of them are mechanical, all of them are findable, and none of them require re-reading the manual.

Why a migration guide is worth more than a style summary

A thesis written under APA 6 does not become APA 7 by changing the heading. The differences are small, numerous and invisible unless you know to look for them. Worse, reference managers keep the style they were given when the database record was created, so a 2016 export can silently carry 2009 punctuation into a 2026 submission. The reliable approach is to migrate once, deliberately, and then check with a tool that flags the old patterns.

Below is every change that affects the average dissertation or journal manuscript, grouped by where you will meet it. Each item gives the old form and the new one.

1. Publisher location is gone

APA 7 removed the city of publication from book and report references. This is the most visible change in a reference list and the easiest to fix in bulk.

APA 6: Smith, J. A. (2016). Doing research well. New York: Sage. APA 7: Smith, J. A. (2016). Doing research well. Sage.

Keep the publisher name exactly as it appears on the copyright page, but drop "New York:", "London:" and any state abbreviation. Do not replace the location with a URL unless you are citing a work you accessed online and your institution asks for the link.

2. "Retrieved from" is gone

APA 6 prefaced web addresses with Retrieved from as a matter of course. APA 7 uses the bare URL. The exception is narrow: when a page is designed to change over time and you need to show when you saw it, give a retrieval date.

APA 6: Retrieved from https://www.example.org/report APA 7: https://www.example.org/report Dynamic source: Retrieved November 10, 2026, from https://www.example.org/live-data

A journal article from a database is not a dynamic source. If it has no DOI, end the entry at the page range and add nothing. The automatic "Retrieved from" in older BibTeX records is one of the highest-yield search-and-replace operations in a migration.

3. Every DOI is a link

APA 7 presents DOIs uniformly as https://doi.org/10.xxxx/.... The label doi:, the bare identifier and the old dx.doi.org redirect are all retired. Do not add a period after the link, because readers will copy the period with the URL.

APA 6: doi: 10.1016/j.jbusres.2016.03.012 APA 7: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2016.03.012

If your reference manager exports doi:10...., change the output style rather than editing 200 entries by hand. Run the finished list through the reference list auditor, which flags the old prefix specifically.

4. Three or more authors: et al. from the start

This is the change people notice first in the text. APA 6 asked you to list up to five authors on first citation and switch to et al. from the sixth. APA 7 uses et al. from the first citation whenever a work has three or more authors.

APA 6 first: (Smith, Lee, Chen, Wang, & Garcia, 2016) APA 6 later: (Smith et al., 2016) APA 7, always: (Smith et al., 2016)

The reference list moved in the opposite direction: it now enumerates up to twenty authors in full, and only with twenty-one or more do you list the first nineteen, an ellipsis, and the final author. A six-author paper under APA 7 has a short in-text citation and a long reference entry. That asymmetry is intentional and catches people who assume the two lists should mirror each other.

5. Bracketed descriptions for non-routine works

APA 7 added square-bracketed labels after the title when the source type is not obvious from the entry. Dissertations, theses, datasets, software, maps and conference presentations are the common cases.

APA 6: Smith, J. A. (2016). Dynamic capabilities in firms [Unpublished doctoral dissertation]. University of X. APA 7: Smith, J. A. (2016). Dynamic capabilities in firms [Doctoral dissertation, University of X]. ProQuest Dissertations and Theses Global.

The bracketed element is not italicised and sits inside the title's sentence, followed by a period. The repository or database name comes after the bracket, not before it. For the full set of dissertation patterns, see citing dissertations and theses in APA 7.

6. Websites, social media and webinars

APA 7 gives online sources their own patterns, which APA 6 handled loosely. A webpage with a named author uses that author; a page with no date gets (n.d.); the site name is italicised unless it is identical to the author. Social-media posts include the platform and the full date, and the URL ends the entry.

Author, A. A. (2024, March 14). Title of the post. Site Name. https://example.org/page

The practical migration task is to check every web entry for three things: a site name that is currently missing, a date that is currently guessed, and a full-width punctuation mark that came from a Chinese-language source.

7. Tables and figures

APA 7 changed the layout of table and figure components. Both now use a bold number on its own line, followed by an italic title on the next line. Notes come below the body in a specific order: general note, specific note, probability note. Statistical and mathematical copy follows a tighter set of rules too, including no leading zero before values that cannot exceed one.

Table 3 Descriptive Statistics for CSR and Firm Performance Note. CSR = corporate social responsibility. n = 280. * p < .05. ** p < .01.

8. A few smaller ones worth catching

  • Sentence case is stated explicitly. Reference titles — article, book, chapter — take sentence case. Database exports in Title Case are the most common surviving APA 6 artefact.
  • Ordinal editions are abbreviated. Write 2nd ed., not Second Edition.
  • URLs no longer need "Accessed". Bare link, no retrieval date, for anything stable.
  • Bias-free language is now a formal expectation, not a courtesy. Person-first or identity-first phrasing should be deliberate and consistent.
  • Level headings are standardised. Level 1 is centred bold title case; level 2 is flush left bold title case; level 3 is flush left bold italic title case.

Do the global replacements in your reference manager's output style first, then paste the result into the reference list auditor. It flags the surviving doi: prefixes, "Retrieved from", "Vol.", "pp." on journal articles, and full-width punctuation — the exact artefacts an APA 6 export leaves behind.

Citing a dissertation or thesis in APA 7

Dissertation entries are where the manual and the institutional template most often disagree. Here is how to build one, what the square brackets actually do, and the six patterns that cover almost every case.

Start with one question: published or not?

APA 7 treats a dissertation that has been made available in a database or institutional repository differently from one that exists only as a bound copy on a shelf. The distinction decides whether your entry ends with a repository name or with an availability phrase.

A published dissertation means it is retrievable by a reader — through ProQuest, an institutional repository, a library catalogue with a stable link, or the university's own archive. It does not mean it went to a commercial press. An unpublished dissertation is one a reader cannot obtain; you cite it with an availability statement and no URL.

The four-element structure

Every dissertation reference is still author, date, title, source. What changes is the source element and the bracketed label attached to the title.

Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the dissertation in sentence case [Doctoral dissertation, Institution Name]. Database or Repository.

Three rules govern this line. First, the title is italicised but the bracketed description is not. Second, the bracket sits immediately after the title, before the period. Third, the institution goes inside the brackets, not after them — a detail carried over from APA 6 that still appears in a lot of thesis templates.

The bracketed description, precisely

The label identifies the type of work when the title alone would not. For a doctorate write [Doctoral dissertation, Institution Name]. For a master's write [Master's thesis, Institution Name]. For a professional doctorate use the degree actually awarded: [Doctor of Business Administration dissertation, Institution Name] is more accurate than the generic form, and accuracy beats brevity in a viva.

Do not italicise the brackets, and do not add the word "Unpublished" if the work is in a database. "Unpublished" belongs to the second pattern below, and using it on a ProQuest record is a straightforward factual error.

Six templates that cover nearly everything

1. Published dissertation in a commercial database

Zhang, W. (2024). Dynamic capabilities and CSR outcomes in Chinese firms [Doctoral dissertation, Golden Gate University]. ProQuest Dissertations and Theses Global. (Publication No. 31204567)

The database name is not italicised. Include the publication number only if the database supplies one and your institution asks for it; it is useful for retrieval but not required by APA 7.

2. Published dissertation in an institutional repository

Chen, L. (2023). Governance of public sector AI adoption [Doctoral dissertation, University of Melbourne]. Institutional Repository of Melbourne. https://repository.example.edu.au/handle/12345/6789

Here the repository name is the source element, followed by the stable link. If the repository itself is the institution, name it once in the brackets and give the link without repeating the name.

3. Unpublished dissertation, reader cannot retrieve it

Smith, J. A. (2020). Leadership and trust in distributed teams [Unpublished doctoral dissertation, University of Somewhere].

No repository, no URL, and no database. The bracket carries both the status and the institution. Use this only when it is true — if the thesis is in a catalogue with a record, it is retrievable and belongs under pattern 1 or 2.

4. Master's thesis

Lee, B. (2022). Employee voice during organisational change [Master's thesis, University of Manchester]. ProQuest Dissertations and Theses Global.

Identical structure, with Master's thesis in the bracket. The apostrophe matters: Master's, not Masters.

5. Dissertation cited from another source

As Wang (2019, as cited in Chen, 2023, p. 41) argued, dynamic capabilities...

Only Chen appears in your reference list. Use this when the original is genuinely inaccessible, then try to obtain it — a chain of dissertations citing dissertations is a common examination question.

6. Citing your own dissertation

Peng, Q. (2026). Dynamic capabilities and CSR: A mechanism study [Doctoral dissertation, Golden Gate University]. ProQuest Dissertations and Theses Global.

You cite your own work exactly as you would anyone else's, in third person, with your full name. Do not write "my dissertation" in the text of a paper derived from it. If the dissertation is still in progress and not yet deposited, cite the individual papers already published from it rather than the thesis itself.

Doctoral dissertation or professional doctorate?

The label should reflect the qualification. If the award is a DBA, EdD or DProf, name it. This matters more than it looks: examiners in professional doctorates often check whether the candidate understands the distinction between a research doctorate and a professional one, and the reference entry is one of the places that shows it.

AwardBracketed description
PhD[Doctoral dissertation, Institution]
DBA[Doctor of Business Administration dissertation, Institution]
EdD[Doctor of Education dissertation, Institution]
MA / MSc by research[Master's thesis, Institution]

The errors that get noticed

  • Institution outside the brackets. APA 7 puts it inside, separated from the degree type by a comma.
  • "Unpublished" on a retrievable thesis. If there is a record, it is published in the APA sense.
  • Title Case in the title. Dissertation titles take sentence case like every other reference title.
  • Italicised brackets. Only the title is italic. The bracket and everything in it is roman type.
  • Database name in italics. It is plain text, unlike a journal name.
  • Missing year or missing institution. Both are mandatory elements.
  • URL pasted after a period. The link ends the entry; adding a full stop after it can break the address.

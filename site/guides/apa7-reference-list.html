Guide · APA 7

The APA 7 reference list, checked line by line

A reference list is not a decoration added at the end. It is the evidence trail for every claim in your manuscript. Here is how the four-element model works, where doctoral writers most often go wrong, and a checklist to run before submission.

Applies to APA 7th edition. Your institution's dissertation template or your target journal's author guidelines take precedence wherever they differ.

The four-element model

Every APA 7 reference answers four questions in a fixed order: Who created it (author), when (date), what is it called (title), and where can a reader find it (source). Once you internalise this, most formatting puzzles become a question about the fourth element rather than a memorisation exercise.

Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (2024). Title of the work in sentence case. Source element, 12(3), 45-67. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/yyyy

The first three elements are nearly identical across source types. Only the source element changes: a journal article needs an italic journal name, italic volume, issue and page range; a book needs an edition and publisher; a chapter needs an editor, book title and chapter pages; a dissertation needs a bracketed description and a repository. That is why a tool organised by source type is more useful than one long template.

Authors: the rules that change with the count

AuthorsReference list format
1Smith, J. A.
2Smith, J. A., & Lee, B. — comma before the ampersand
3 to 20Smith, J. A., Lee, B., & Chen, C. — serial comma, then ampersand
21 or moreFirst 19 authors, an ellipsis, then the final author, with no ampersand
Group authorSpell out the organisation in full; do not abbreviate in the reference list
No authorMove the title into the author position, then the date

Surnames keep their original particles: "van der Berg" stays together, so enter it as van der Berg, J. rather than letting software split it. Suffixes such as "Jr." follow the initials, separated by a comma. A common failure mode in doctoral theses is the 21-author rule applied wrongly: the ellipsis is not "et al.", and the last author is still named in full.

Date, title and sentence case

Use the year in parentheses. For web content and conference presentations a fuller date such as (2024, May 4) is appropriate when the source supplies one. Use (n.d.) only when no date can be found, not when you did not look. Use (in press) only after the article has been accepted.

Reference titles take sentence case: capitalise the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns. Journal names, by contrast, keep Title Case and are italicised. This asymmetry is deliberate and trips up almost everyone who exports from a bibliographic database, because databases store article titles in Title Case. A reliable workflow is to export, then convert article titles to sentence case by hand while leaving journal names untouched. Automatic converters will lowercase "Structural Equation Modeling" and "Federal Reserve" with equal enthusiasm.

Bracketed descriptions such as [Doctoral dissertation], [Conference presentation] or [Data set] sit immediately after the title, are not italicised, and identify the type of work when it is not obvious. They are one of the more visible changes between APA 6 and APA 7.

Italics: what is and is not italicised

  • Italicise: journal name, journal volume number, book title, edited book title in a chapter reference, dissertation title, website name (when different from the author), conference paper title.
  • Do not italicise: issue number in parentheses, article title, chapter title, edition statement, publisher name, page range, DOI or URL.

A useful sanity check: the italicised element is the container, not the individual work inside it. The article title is plain while the journal is italic; the chapter title is plain while the book is italic.

DOIs and URLs

Every DOI is presented as a link: https://doi.org/10.xxxx/yyyy. No "doi:" label, no "Retrieved from", no terminal period. If an article has a DOI, include it even when you read a print version. If an article has no DOI and came from an academic database, end the reference at the page range — do not add the database URL. DOI-less articles that exist openly on the web may include a URL.

APA 7 removed publisher locations from book references, so New York: Wiley becomes simply Wiley. It also dropped "Retrieved from" except where a retrieval date is genuinely needed for content designed to change, such as a live statistics dashboard or an archived dataset.

Worked example: from metadata to entry

Suppose your reference manager gives you this raw record: authors "Smith, John A." and "Lee, B."; year 2024; title "Dynamic Capabilities and Firm Performance"; journal "Journal of Business Research"; volume 142; issue 3; pages 45-67; DOI "doi:10.1016/j.jbusres.2023.114321".

  1. Reduce given names to initials and join with the ampersand: Smith, J. A., & Lee, B.
  2. Put the year in parentheses: (2024).
  3. Convert the title to sentence case: Dynamic capabilities and firm performance.
  4. Build the source element: Journal of Business Research, 142(3), 45-67. with the journal name and "142" in italics and "(3)" in roman type.
  5. Normalise the DOI and add no final period.
Smith, J. A., & Lee, B. (2024). Dynamic capabilities and firm performance. Journal of Business Research, 142(3), 45-67. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2023.114321

Pre-submission checklist

  1. Every in-text citation has a reference-list entry, and every entry is cited in the text.
  2. Entries are alphabetised by the first author's surname, letter by letter.
  3. The list is double-spaced with a hanging indent of 0.5 inch, with no extra blank lines between entries.
  4. Article titles are in sentence case; journal names are in Title Case and italicised, along with volume numbers.
  5. All DOIs are https://doi.org/... links with no trailing period.
  6. No "Retrieved from", no "Vol.", no "pp." on journal articles, no "et al." in the list.
  7. Group authors are spelled out; no unexplained abbreviations.
  8. Punctuation is half-width, and there is exactly one space after each punctuation mark.
  9. The whole list matches the dissertation template or the journal's author guidelines where they override APA 7.

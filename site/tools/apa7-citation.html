How it works

What the builder does, and what it deliberately refuses to do

APA 7 reference entries follow a four-element structure: author, date, title, and source. The builder assembles those four elements in the right order for the source type you select, because the fourth element is the one that changes most: a journal article needs an italic journal title and volume, a book needs a publisher, a chapter needs an "In" clause with editors and page range, and a dissertation needs a bracketed description plus its repository.

Author handling

Names entered as "Smith, John A." are reduced to "Smith, J. A."; names entered as "John A. Smith" are inverted first. Hyphenated given names such as "Jean-Luc" become "J.-L." For a compound surname or a suffix such as "Jr.", use the Surname, Given form so the tool does not split the surname. The tool does not attempt to detect nobility particles, particles such as "van der", or organisation names with internal commas — enter those carefully and proofread the result.

The number of authors changes the punctuation. One author ends with a period. Two authors are joined with , & . Three to twenty authors use a serial comma before the ampersand. With twenty-one or more, APA 7 lists the first nineteen, inserts an ellipsis, and then gives the final author with no ampersand; the builder implements that rule and tells you when it fires.

Sentence case is your decision

Reference titles take sentence case: capitalise the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns. Converting Title Case automatically sounds easy but fails on acronyms, chemical names, proper nouns and discipline-specific capitalisation (for example "Dynamic Capabilities" may be a named theoretical construct in your field). The builder therefore preserves the title exactly as you type it and raises a warning when it detects three or more capitalised words that look like Title Case or ALL CAPS. Export tools and bibliographic databases commonly deliver Title Case; this is the single most common thing to fix after an import.

Italics, DOIs and URLs

For journal articles, the journal name and the volume number are italicised; the issue number in parentheses is not. For books and dissertations the title is italicised, and for web pages the site name is italicised unless it is identical to the author. A DOI is normalised to https://doi.org/... whether you paste the bare identifier, a doi: prefix, a dx.doi.org link or the final URL. No terminal period is added after a DOI or URL because it tends to be copied as part of the link.

If a journal article genuinely has no DOI, APA 7 says to end the entry at the page range when the article came from an academic database; do not add the database URL. Add a URL only for a webpage-style source or where your institution explicitly requires it.

Privacy and verification

The builder is a short JavaScript file with no dependencies and no network calls. You can read it, you can use it offline, and you can test the formatting rules yourself against the manual. If your institution uses a modified house style, treat the output as a starting point rather than a final answer.

Worked example

Given two authors (Smith, John A. and Lee, B.), year 2024, title Dynamic capabilities and firm performance, journal Journal of Business Research, volume 142, issue 3, pages 45-67, and DOI 10.1016/j.jbusres.2023.114321, the expected output is:

Smith, J. A., & Lee, B. (2024). Dynamic capabilities and firm performance. Journal of Business Research, 142(3), 45-67. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2023.114321

In a manuscript the journal name and the "142" are italicised, and the DOI is a live link. Use "Load example" above to reproduce this directly in the tool.

How the parser works

It is a deterministic rule engine, not a language model. It looks for the four APA elements in order — author, date, title, source — then hands the recovered fields to the same formatter used by the reference builder. Because the logic is a set of explicit rules, you can inspect it, predict it, and disagree with it. Nothing is sent to a server, and nothing is stored.

What it refuses to guess

The parser never rewrites your title into sentence case. Automatic conversion looks attractive and quietly destroys acronyms, proper nouns and named theoretical constructs — "Structural Equation Modeling", "Federal Reserve", "Dynamic Capabilities". Instead it preserves the title exactly and raises a warning when three or more capitalised words suggest Title Case or ALL CAPS. Changing those words is a scholarly decision, and it stays yours.

It also does not verify that a DOI resolves, that a journal exists, or that the cited source supports the claim in your text. No formatting tool can do that.

Known limits

  • Best results come from one reference per line. A wrapped, multi-line entry will be treated as two entries.
  • Chapter and conference entries with complex "In ... (Ed.)" structures often need manual field entry in the builder.
  • Compound surnames and suffixes should still use the Surname, Given form.
  • A confidence score is shown per entry. Read it as "how many fields were found", not "how correct the reference is".

What it checks

Fourteen rules, and why each one exists

The auditor is a rule-based heuristic, not an AI and not a style authority. It looks for surface patterns that reliably indicate an APA 7 problem. That makes it fast, private and predictable — but it also means it can miss a genuinely wrong entry, and it can occasionally flag a legitimate one. Use it as a proofreading pass before you do the manual check, never instead of it.

Errors: almost always wrong

FlagRule
doi: prefix or dx.doi.orgAPA 7 renders every DOI as https://doi.org/10.xxxx/.... Older styles and older versions of reference managers still emit the prefixed form.
Authors joined with "and"In a reference list the last two authors are joined with &. "and" is correct only for narrative in-text citations.
et al. in the listReference lists enumerate up to twenty authors. et al. is an in-text shorthand.
Vol. or VolumeThe volume number follows the journal title directly, in italics, with no label.
Missing parenthesised yearThe date element is mandatory, in parentheses. Use (n.d.) when there is genuinely no date.
Full-width punctuationChinese commas, periods and parentheses break citation parsers and are invisible when scanning by eye. This matters for bilingual theses.

Warnings: check the source before changing anything

FlagRule
"Retrieved from"APA 7 drops it unless the source is designed to change over time and a retrieval date is meaningful, such as an archived dataset or a live statistics page.
pp. before pagesCorrect for a book chapter, wrong for a journal article. The auditor cannot tell the two apart reliably, so it warns instead of asserting.
Title Case or ALL CAPSReference titles use sentence case. BibTeX exports and database downloads frequently arrive in Title Case.
Ampersand without a preceding commaWith three or more authors, the serial comma before & is required.
Double spacesOne space after punctuation.
Period after a URLA full stop at the end of a link is often copied as part of the URL and breaks it.
"Available from" / "Accessed on"Wording from other styles, most often Vancouver or Harvard variants.
No terminal periodEntries that do not end with a link should end with a period.

What it cannot see

Italics are invisible in plain text, so the auditor cannot check whether a journal name or volume is italicised. It cannot verify that a DOI resolves, that a title really is sentence case when proper nouns are involved, that an issue number belongs to that volume, or that the source type you chose matches the source you actually used. It also cannot detect a missing author if the entry still contains a year. These are the items to check manually against the manual and against your own PDFs.

A practical audit sequence

  1. Export your reference list from your manager as plain text, one entry per line. Do not export the formatted bibliography with hanging indents and blank lines — the auditor treats each non-empty line as one entry.
  2. Run the audit and clear the errors first. Most of them come from a single outdated export style, so a global find-and-replace in your manager often fixes many lines at once.
  3. Read the warnings against the source. A pp. on a book chapter is correct; a pp. on a journal article is not.
  4. Paste the cleaned list back into your manuscript and check italics, hanging indent, alphabetical order and double spacing by eye.
  5. Cross-check every in-text citation has a list entry, and every list entry is cited. No formatting tool can do that for you.
